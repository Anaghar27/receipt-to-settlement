## Issues caught while checking AI output

- The first draft of the dinner sample had the header `TABLE 4 - GUESTS 3`. It ends in a number, so the
  parser would have read it as a $3.00 item. I reworded it to `TABLE FOUR, PARTY OF THREE`.
- The first `price_to_cents` was only safe behind the parser's regex. `"4.5"` returned 405 instead of
  450, and `"$4.49"` crashed. This was found by probing it with inputs outside the regex, then fixed and
  covered by tests.
- Rounding conflict: the spec rounds each person's total, but the breakdown must also be in whole
  cents. With pre-tax = total minus tax minus tip, a brute-force search found a pre-tax amount of minus
  $0.01 in 1,920 of 200,000 extreme receipts (tax + tip as large as the items) and in 0 of 200,000
  realistic ones. Totals are never negative. Decision (made by me after the AI flagged it): keep this
  design, with tests pinning both facts.
- The first UI showed a "Line" column (starting at 2, because line 1 is the header) and messages like
  "Line 6 may be $11.87". My call: line numbers are an internal ID, so every message and table now names
  the product ("CHICKEN BREAST may be $11.87…"). `line_no` is still used internally for assignments and
  highlighting. This departs from the "Line 5 may be…" wording in CLAUDE.md on purpose.
- Found by me in the browser, missed by the AI's headless checks: Streamlit renders text as Markdown,
  so "Items sum to $165.89 but the subtotal is $65.89" turned the part between the two `$` signs into a
  math formula. Fixed with `no_math()` in `app.py`, which escapes `$` in every message shown, and an
  `AppTest` check now confirms no rendered message has an unescaped `$`. Lesson: `AppTest` checks text,
  not looks.
- The AI's own looser "first word" rule for summary labels backfired on a real receipt. "Total Taxes
  $3.67" became the TOTAL, and "Grand Total" and "Amount Due" became items. It was replaced with
  whole-word matching in priority order.
- Real photo, tested by hand (a stock restaurant receipt, not stored in the repo): Tesseract garbled two
  of four item lines. Validation blocked settlement with "an item worth $25.90 may be missing", which is
  exactly those two items, and fixing them in the text box let it settle. I tried letting the parser
  ignore stray characters after a price (the "$16.95 a" case) and rejected it, because it turned ID
  numbers into items.
- Two of the AI's own fixes were wrong on first try, and running them on real photos caught both. Always
  enlarging small photos made the Nello receipt worse (measured, 12/20 vs 15/20), so it was replaced by
  "read both, keep the reading that checks out". The first "subtotal may be misread" hint was wrong for El
  Chalan, where OCR had lost two items, so the hint now gives both possible causes.
- I asked whether quantities were checked when the Nello totals did not match. They were not. The check
  was silently skipped (11 quantity lines, limit 10), because the AI had made it report only when it
  changed something. It now always reports what it checked.
- `app.py` rebuilt the `Receipt` field by field after table edits, so a newly added field
  (`summary_raw`) was silently dropped and the quoted hint did not appear in the UI, even though
  the unit tests passed. This was caught by the `AppTest` check and fixed with `dataclasses.replace`.
- Plain `pytest -q` could not import the `fairsplit` package. Adding `pytest.ini` with `pythonpath = .`
  fixed it.
